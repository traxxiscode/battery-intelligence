Status Logic

  • Needs Attention: used for sustained recent weakness, not one-off dips. It typically requires a low recent average or multiple warning signals together, such as a high weak-read rate combined with worsening trend.
  • Watch List: used when early warning signs are present but the overall recent average is still fairly normal. This is often the right status for vehicles that show occasional weak starts or mild deterioration.
  • Healthy: shown when the recent average, weak-read rate, and recent trend do not suggest a sustained problem.
  • Weak read: any cranking-voltage reading at or below 10.2 V during the recent 45-day window.
  • Deep low events: repeated reads at or below 9.4 V add weight, but they do not create a Needs Attention status by themselves. They need to line up with the recent average, the share of weak reads, or weak starts during normal use.
  • Inactivity context: low-voltage events are compared with trip gaps. If most weak reads happen after 7 or more idle days and very few happen during normal use, we treat the result more cautiously and explain that in the review.
  • Recent shift: if the last few weeks look meaningfully worse than the couple of weeks before that, the review notes an estimated date when the sharper change appears to begin.
  • Vehicle review: the detailed view turns the data into a plain-English summary, shows the recent voltage pattern, highlights usage activity, and gives you a printable export.
